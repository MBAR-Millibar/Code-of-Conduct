# Millibar.io Code of Conduct for Diving Professionals

> A voluntary commitment for diving professionals and businesses. It builds on
> the [Millibar.io Code of Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md), which applies to
> everyone in Millibar spaces.

## Why this code

Diving is built on trust. Students and guests put their safety in the hands of
professionals, in places where physical contact, travel, shared boats and
accommodation, and depending on someone else are normal. That creates real
differences in power, and with them a real responsibility.

A healthy diving industry is one where everyone feels safe to learn, work and
dive. This code describes what that looks like in practice, and invites every
diving professional to commit to it publicly.

## Who it is for

All diving professionals: instructors, assistant instructors, divemasters, dive
guides, freediving instructors, trainees and interns, and the dive centres,
resorts, liveaboards and other businesses that employ them, including their
staff and crew.

## How it works

Signing this code is voluntary. Any diving professional or business can sign it,
and signatories can show it on their Millibar profile. By signing, you commit to
following this code in your work, and to making it known to your students,
guests, staff and colleagues.

The [Millibar.io Code of Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) applies to everyone who
uses Millibar, including signatories. This code adds commitments for diving
work: in the water, on boats, on trips and during training.

## Respect and inclusion

- Welcome every diver, regardless of age, body, visible or invisible
  disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression,
  experience, nationality, religion (or lack of religion), or sexual
  orientation.
- Adapt teaching and equipment to different bodies, abilities and ways of
  learning.
- Never make demeaning comments about someone's body, fitness, skill or
  background.
- Encourage questions, and never ridicule mistakes. Everyone was a beginner
  once.

## Consent and physical contact

- Limit physical contact to what is needed for safety and training.
- Before a session, explain what contact may happen and why, and ask. Where
  possible, offer alternatives, such as demonstrating on yourself or letting the
  diver adjust their own equipment.
- Respect a "no". Outside a real emergency, there is no exception.
- Respect privacy when people change, shower or put on wetsuits.
- Take photos or videos of people, in or out of the water, only with their
  consent, and share them only with their permission.

## Power and influence

- Never use influence over certifications, sign-offs, access to trips, jobs,
  internships, reviews or visibility to seek personal, romantic or sexual
  favours.
- Do not make romantic or sexual advances toward students, guests or trainees
  while you are responsible for them.
- Agree any exchange arrangement (e.g. work for training, content for trips,
  internships) in writing. It must be fair, and it never depends on anything
  personal.
- Give staff, interns and volunteers clear, written terms for their work, pay
  and accommodation.

## Trips, boats and accommodation

- No pressure around alcohol, accommodation or after-hours socialising.
- Never require a student, guest or trainee to share a room with a professional
  who is responsible for them.
- Anyone can leave a situation that feels unsafe, without consequences for
  their training, trip or work. Help them get somewhere safe or contact local
  authorities if they ask.

## A culture of safety and speaking up

- Any diver can call off a dive, or skip a skill, at any time, without being
  shamed.
- Look out for your students, guests and colleagues, and speak up when
  something is wrong.
- Support people who raise a concern. Never retaliate against them.

## Handling concerns in your business

As a signatory, you commit to:

- Naming a person in your business that students, guests and staff can go to
  with a concern, and making sure they know who it is.
- Listening first, taking every concern seriously, and handling it
  confidentially.
- Focusing on learning and repairing harm, as described in the
  [Millibar.io Code of Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md#addressing-and-repairing-harm),
  while always putting the safety of the person affected first.
- Involving the authorities where appropriate, with the wishes of the person
  affected in mind.

## Raising a concern with Millibar

Anyone can raise a concern about a signatory by writing to
**[conduct@millibar.io](mailto:conduct@millibar.io)**. Millibar is not an
authority over independent professionals and businesses, but we will make every
possible effort to keep the industry healthy:

- We listen, and help the person affected find support or contact the
  authorities.
- We talk with the professional or business, to understand what happened and
  help put it right.
- If a signatory does not follow this code, we remove their signatory status.
  Where their behaviour also breaks the Millibar.io Code of Conduct, we may limit
  or end their presence and partnerships on Millibar.

We acknowledge concerns within 48 hours, handle them confidentially, and there is
no retaliation for raising a concern in good faith.

## Fair process

If you are a signatory and believe a concern about you was handled unfairly,
write to [conduct@millibar.io](mailto:conduct@millibar.io) with a short
description. Both sides are heard before a final decision, and everyone involved
is treated with respect.

## Review

We review this code at least once a year, together with signatories, and after
any concern that shows it needs to change.
